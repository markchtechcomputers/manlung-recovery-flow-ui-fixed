Scammed? You are not alone.

Lost money to a scam? Take these steps now.

Act quickly to protect your accounts, preserve evidence and report the incident. There is no shame in being scammed—and no legitimate service can guarantee that stolen money will be recovered.

Beware of recovery scams

People may pose as police, government officials, lawyers, banks, charities or “recovery agents”. They may demand an upfront fee, tax, deposit, crypto payment or remote access to your device. Stop contact and do not pay them.

01

Contact your bank or payment provider

Call using the number in the official app, card or website. Report the transaction immediately and ask whether it can be stopped, recalled or disputed. If mobile money was used, contact your mobile operator through its verified official channel.

02

Secure your accounts

Change exposed passwords from a trusted device, start with email and financial accounts, enable two-factor authentication, sign out other sessions and contact your provider if your SIM or account was taken over. Never share PINs or one-time codes.

03

Save the evidence

Keep transaction IDs, receipts, phone numbers, usernames, URLs, emails, chat screenshots and dates. Do not delete conversations or send your original evidence to strangers. Share copies only through trusted reporting channels.

04

Report it to official channels

Report the crime to the police/DCI and consider reporting the digital incident to Kenya’s National KE-CIRT/CC. Use the official links below and verify the destination before sharing information.

Warning signs of a second scam

  • Someone contacts you unexpectedly after your first loss and promises guaranteed recovery.
  • They ask for an advance fee, “release charge”, tax, insurance, deposit or a percentage paid before recovery.
  • They claim they have already located your funds but need more money to continue.
  • They impersonate a government agency, lawyer, police officer, bank, charity or a past victim who “got a refund”.
  • They ask for your banking login, M-Pesa PIN, one-time password, seed phrase, identity documents without a verified need, or remote access to your phone/computer.
