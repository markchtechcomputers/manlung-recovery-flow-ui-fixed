🔄 Recovery

Recovery is often a process rather than a single event. Start by understanding what happened and organizing the information available to you.

Start here

  1. Write down what happened and when it happened.
  2. Preserve messages, emails, screenshots, receipts and other relevant records.
  3. Identify accounts, devices, transactions or information affected.
  4. Record what action has already been taken.
  5. Define the outcome you are trying to achieve.

Common recovery situations

  • Lost or stolen devices
  • Compromised accounts
  • Online scams and fraud
  • Identity-related problems
  • Loss of important digital information

Important reminder

Recovery cannot always guarantee a particular outcome. The available options depend on the situation, evidence, service providers and applicable authorities.

🤝 Support

Good support begins with clear information and realistic expectations.

Preparing to ask for help

  • Explain what happened in chronological order.
  • Provide relevant dates and transaction references.
  • Identify affected accounts, devices or services.
  • Keep original evidence where possible.
  • Clearly explain what assistance you are seeking.

What happens after a request?

Your information can be reviewed so that the appropriate next steps can be identified. Some matters may require a bank, platform, telecommunications provider, police service or other qualified professional.

Should I share everything publicly?

No. Sensitive personal, financial and account information should be shared only through appropriate trusted channels.

📚 Education

Understanding basic digital safety can reduce risk and help you respond more effectively when something goes wrong.

Digital safety basics

  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
  • Keep devices and applications updated.
  • Review account recovery methods regularly.
  • Be cautious with unexpected links and attachments.

Understanding digital evidence

Messages, emails, transaction records, timestamps, screenshots and account notifications may help establish what happened. Avoid altering or deleting useful records unnecessarily.

🌍 Community

People often handle difficult situations better when they know where to find trustworthy information and appropriate support.

How to support someone

  • Listen without blaming them.
  • Respect their privacy.
  • Help them organize information.
  • Encourage appropriate professional assistance.
  • Avoid making promises about outcomes.

Responsible information sharing

Do not publish passwords, private documents, financial information, phone numbers or other sensitive evidence simply to seek community assistance.

🔎 Awareness

Awareness can help you recognize suspicious situations earlier.

Common warning signs

  • Unexpected requests for passwords or verification codes.
  • Pressure to send money immediately.
  • Promises of guaranteed profits or guaranteed recovery.
  • Suspicious links or unfamiliar login pages.
  • Requests to keep a transaction secret.
  • Someone pretending to be a bank, company or official.

If you suspect a scam

Stop communicating or paying where appropriate, preserve evidence, contact the relevant financial institution or platform, and use official reporting channels.

🌱 Resilience

Resilience can mean continuing to take constructive steps after a setback.

Build safer routines

  • Review important account security settings regularly.
  • Keep backups of important information.
  • Maintain records of important transactions.
  • Learn from previous incidents without blaming yourself.
  • Ask for qualified help when the situation requires it.

Progress after a setback

A setback does not erase every positive step already taken. Review what changed, adjust your approach and focus on the next useful action.
